## Research foundation

- Zhang et al. (2024), [Mind In Vitro Platforms: Versatile, Scalable, Robust, and Open Solutions to Interfacing with Living Neurons](https://doi.org/10.1002/advs.202306826), Advanced Science. Custom MEAs and modular interfaces for in-vitro electrophysiology. The published system is not automatically equivalent to a turnkey long-term bioreactor.
- Kagan et al. (2022), [In vitro neurons learn and exhibit sentience when embodied in a simulated game-world](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2022.09.001), Neuron. DishBrain uses closed-loop stimulation and recording in simplified Pong. Its title's sentience interpretation is not presented as an established conclusion in the slide.
- Habibollahi et al. (2023), [Critical dynamics arise during structured information presentation within embodied in vitro neuronal networks](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-41020-3), Nature Communications. Follow-up on network dynamics.
- Khajehnejad et al. (2024), [Biological Neurons Compete with Deep Reinforcement Learning in Sample Efficiency in a Simulated Gameworld](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16946), preprint. Comparison under constrained, time-matched task conditions, not general superiority to AI.
- [Local maker account](https://eigenlucy.com/projects/MindInVitro/) describes replication and revision of MiV with people at Frontier Tower and Neuromorph VC; it does not independently establish Berkeley's facility arrangements or completed learning experiments.

## Morgan's current project account

Morgan reports collaboration with Open Ephys toward a low-cost MEA/bioreactor and that Neurotech@Berkeley's wetware group uses Biopunk Lab for cell culture, enabling replication work informed by DishBrain and follow-ups. These statements are attributed to Morgan, not inferred from public documentation. No claim of completed replication, demonstrated learning, formal university partnership, or clinical capability. MiV hardware replication and DishBrain experimental replication are distinct tasks.
