\begin{frame}{MOABB: making BCI comparisons reproducible}
  \textbf{Mother of All BCI Benchmarks}\par\medskip
  An open framework for comparing algorithms on public EEG datasets.\par\bigskip
  \textbf{A concrete research contribution}\par\medskip
  The 2024 benchmark study evaluated 30 machine-learning pipelines\\
  across 36 datasets, covering motor imagery, P300, and SSVEP.\par\bigskip
  Shared implementations and evaluation methods give other\\
  researchers a starting point they can inspect and reproduce.
  \NTXSource{https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.15319}{Chevallier et al., 2024, arXiv:2404.15319}
  \note{[8:00--10:00] MOABB is an example of community work producing a research artifact beyond a single event. The numerical scope is specific to the cited 2024 study, not today's dataset total. Credit the contributors. Do not generalize the study into a claim that a particular algorithm always wins or that benchmark performance establishes clinical utility.}
\end{frame}

\section{What does the future hold for neurotech founders?}
\begin{frame}{Founders can build on work already shared}
  \NTXPoint{Learn the field}{Use educational resources and community conversations to develop a working understanding.}
  \NTXPoint{Explore an idea}{Use open experiments and tools to investigate a technical question.}
  \NTXPoint{Test the claim}{Use reproducible baselines to ask what an approach actually adds.}
  \NTXSource{https://github.com/NeuroTechX/moabb}{Founder-facing implications of the resources shown; MOABB and EEG-ExPy}
  \note{[18:00--20:00] This is the transition to the future, grounded in resources that exist. It is a suggested use of community resources, not a new NeuroTechX program or a promise of company formation. Open projects can inform exploration, but an intended product must establish its own requirements and evidence. No licensing or commercial-use advice is implied.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Where could the next founder contribute?}
  \NTXPoint{Tools for people doing neurotechnology}{Recording workflows, experiments, analysis, and reproducibility.}
  \NTXPoint{Applications built around a specific need}{The recent hackathon explored speech interfaces, BCI games, and open-ended prototypes.}
  \NTXPoint{The practical questions}{Who needs this? What works today? What evidence is still missing?}
  \NTXSource{https://global-neurohack.github.io/}{Global NeuroHack 2026 tracks; application questions for discussion}
  \note{[20:00--22:00] Present these as directions to explore, not predictions of market size, timelines, or technical readiness. In particular, a non-implantable speech-decoding challenge does not establish a working speech product. A game built with mock neural data is not a demonstrated human BCI. Keep the distinction between a project prompt and a product claim clear.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{A founder can also be a community contributor}
  \NTXPoint{Bring a question people can work on}{A technical problem, an experiment, or a result to examine together.}
  \NTXPoint{Share something useful}{Documentation, a reproducible example, a workshop, or time helping another builder.}
  \NTXPoint{Build relationships through the work}{Meet people by learning, making, and contributing alongside them.}
  \note{[22:00--24:00] This is an invitation to participate, grounded in the existing formats. It does not promise cofounder matching, funding, regulatory support, or access to a particular partner. Invite founders to think about what they can add to the community as well as what they hope to find. This is discussion framing, not an organizational commitment.}
\end{frame}
